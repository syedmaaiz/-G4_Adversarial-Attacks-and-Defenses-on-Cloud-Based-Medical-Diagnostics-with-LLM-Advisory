Executive Summary

The baseline model worked on normal images, but FGSM exposed a real weakness. Adversarial training was the strongest defense.

Recommended defense: adversarial training It raised attacked-image accuracy from 53.69% to 74.04% and reduced attack success from 37.38% to 13.81%, while clean accuracy stayed essentially unchanged.

What We Did In Simple Words

A non-technical walkthrough of the project result.

Step 1

We trained a normal X-ray classifier

The baseline model learned to classify chest X-rays as NORMAL or PNEUMONIA. Before any attack, it was correct 85.74% of the time.

Step 2

We attacked the model

FGSM added tiny calculated changes to the X-ray images. After the attack, baseline accuracy fell to 53.69%, meaning many predictions were fooled.

Step 3

A simple defense helped only a little

Defensive randomization slightly changed images before prediction. It improved attacked-image accuracy to 57.05%, but that was only a small recovery.

Step 4

Training with attacked images helped a lot

Adversarial training taught the model using clean and attacked images. Attacked-image accuracy improved to 74.04% while clean accuracy stayed about the same.
